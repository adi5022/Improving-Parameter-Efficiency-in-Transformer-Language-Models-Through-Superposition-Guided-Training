### Important findings
1. Trained LMs are already in the strong-superposition regime, which explains their ~1/width loss scaling (SUP_2025_002, NeurIPS 2025). The README's "optimise superposition for more capacity per parameter" has no positive evidence and a strong counter-argument.
2. Interpretable-by-design training (SoLU, weight-sparse, MoE-X, Monet, codebook) gives interpretability at equal or lower capability — weight-sparse models need 100–1,000× compute for equal capability.
3. Published claims disagree on the sign of the monosemanticity–performance effect (DecPO encourage vs L2E inhibit); evidence on both sides is weak (no seeds/variance in what was read).
4. Interpretability already influences training in fine-tuning (CAFT, preventative steering, LoRA-to-fit-SAE). Not found: an online SAE driving an auxiliary loss during from-scratch pretraining, compared against non-MI regularisers and random dictionaries — candidate gap G1.
5. SAE signals are unstable (~30% feature overlap across seeds), suffer absorption, lose to simple baselines, and auto-interp scores do not separate trained from random models. The user's own FeatureScalpel results agree (SAE helps but is not required: 95% vs 81% random basis).
6. Distillation: capacity gap; gpt-oss-120b's 201,088-token vocabulary makes logit KD into a small student impractical; sequence-level KD confounds data with method.
7. Training-time activation changes demonstrably improve quantisation (clipped softmax; OSP, ACL 2025); quantisation degrades SAE features even when perplexity is unchanged (2026 preprint). The quantisation-robustness hypothesis is the best-grounded part of the README.
8. Formal point recorded: at the optimum of the LM training loss, an added regulariser cannot lower that loss; any gain must come from optimisation dynamics, generalisation, or properties the loss does not measure. All candidate hypotheses now name their mechanism.

## Addendum — discussion later in session 1 (2026-11-11)

**What happened:** the user reviewed the assessment and corrected it. The first assessment had partly read the proposal as being about interpretability and reducing interference. The user's actual intent: push the model to superimpose **more** concepts per dimension than standard training ("sacrificing interpretability for storage"), so a smaller model stores as much as a larger one.

**User statements recorded (not yet formal approvals of wording):**
- Penalty/reward is a loss term inside the training loop, not a separate reward model.
- SAE reconstruction loss is considered; anything inserted into the forward pass uses delta patching only.
- Quantisation stays: our model (≈ 2 GB → ≈ 1 GB) vs standard model (≈ 3 GB → ≈ 1.5 GB); similar performance = success.
- Toy model first, then small LMs.
- Smaller teacher for distillation (later).
- Reuse standard components; focus on the training path.
- Proposal: train the SAE jointly in the same loop so it keeps matching the model.

**My response (advice, not decisions):** joint SAE training is workable as a two-objective loop (SAE updated on its own reconstruction/sparsity loss with detached activations; model updated on LM loss + penalty with the SAE held fixed). Penalty gradients must not reach the SAE, or it learns to report more superposition instead of measuring it. Recommended a reconstruction-quality gate and an independent ruler (ground truth in the toy model; fresh SAEs on LM checkpoints). Flagged: superposition must be measured per dimension, not per neuron; higher per-token L0 is not more stored features; denser packing may be more quantisation-fragile; weight decay is the mandatory cheap baseline.

**Reviewed and not adopted — pasted web-search answer on "forcing superposition" (2026-11-11):** an uncited, AI-style summary suggesting combinatorial/blended training data, concept dropout, mixup, low-rank bottlenecks, shared cross-attention, label smoothing, an embedding-alignment loss (1 − cos(z_A, z_B)) and attention-entropy maximisation. Assessment: it uses "superposition" in the sense of *blending concepts in outputs* (mostly image/diffusion), not the mechanistic sense (many sparse features stored as nearly-orthogonal directions in fewer dimensions). The alignment loss is the opposite of the goal: it merges two features into one direction, which maximises interference. Data blending and label smoothing change the data/targets, not storage per dimension. One correct point: superposition rises when capacity shrinks — but that is what a smaller standard model already does, so it is the baseline, not a method; it also shows that "amount of superposition" alone cannot be the success measure (loss at fixed width must be). Not added to the citation index (no primary source).
